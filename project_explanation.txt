AEGIS RISK INTELLIGENCE
PROJECT EXPLANATION

1. What the Web Application Does

Aegis Risk Intelligence is a fraud detection and transaction investigation web application. It is designed for financial crime teams, fraud analysts, risk teams, and security operations teams.

The application watches financial transactions and highlights activity that may be fraudulent or unusual. It gives analysts a central place to see risk levels, understand why a transaction was flagged, investigate a case, and decide what action should be taken.

The application is useful for:

- Finding suspicious transfers and other unusual transactions.
- Prioritizing the cases that need attention first.
- Understanding the reasons behind a fraud warning.
- Reviewing account balances before and after a transaction.
- Checking whether a recipient account has a suspicious history.
- Freezing an account when immediate protection is needed.
- Resolving a legitimate case or marking a warning as a false positive.
- Escalating serious cases to senior investigators.
- Testing hypothetical transactions before they are approved.
- Monitoring fraud trends, losses, and model performance.
- Managing risk rules and review thresholds.
- Exporting transaction information for reporting and compliance work.

2. Who Uses It

The main user is a fraud or financial crime analyst. An analyst can use the application to monitor incoming alerts, investigate individual transactions, record decisions, and protect customers from suspicious transfers.

Managers and risk engineers can use the overview and model pages to monitor the overall fraud situation, review detection performance, and adjust risk policies.

3. Main Pages and Their Uses

3.1 Executive Overview

The Executive Overview is the main command center. It gives a quick picture of the current fraud situation.

It shows:

- The total number of transactions processed.
- The number of transactions flagged as suspicious.
- The current active investigation queue.
- The estimated financial exposure from risky activity.
- Live security alerts that still need attention.
- Recent changes in fraud volume and expected loss.
- The types of transaction channels involved in fraud.
- A short list of the most important cases waiting for review.

The overview is useful at the beginning of a shift or during an incident because it helps an analyst understand the current workload without opening every case.

3.2 Fraud Triage Queue

The Fraud Triage Queue is the analyst's worklist. It contains transactions that have been identified as suspicious or require a decision.

An analyst can:

- Search for a case by case ID, account, or reason.
- Filter cases by fraud category.
- Filter by risk level.
- Filter by transaction channel, such as transfer or cash withdrawal.
- View active, frozen, resolved, and false-positive cases.
- Compare transaction amount, velocity, structuring, recipient risk, and final risk.
- Open a case for a detailed investigation.
- Freeze an account when the activity appears dangerous.
- Resolve a case when the transaction is legitimate or handled.
- Mark a case as a false positive for future model improvement.
- Refresh the queue to see the latest stored case information.
- Export the queue data as a CSV report.

The queue helps analysts work in priority order instead of manually searching through all transactions.

3.3 Case Investigator

The Case Investigator is the detailed view for one transaction. It is used when an analyst needs to understand the full story behind a fraud alert.

It presents:

- The case ID, current status, risk score, and risk level.
- The sender and recipient account information.
- The sender balance before and after the transaction.
- The recipient balance before and after the transaction.
- The transaction amount and channel.
- Whether the sender account was emptied.
- Whether the transaction is unusually large.
- Recent transaction counts and amounts.
- Recipient behavior and reputation information.
- The main factors that contributed to the risk decision.
- A history of previous analyst actions and notes.

From this page, the analyst can:

- Freeze the originating account.
- Resolve the case.
- Mark the case as a false positive.
- Escalate the case for higher-level review.
- Add investigation notes and assign responsibility.
- Print or save a case investigation dossier.

This page supports a documented and explainable decision instead of relying only on a warning label.

3.4 Model Intelligence

Model Intelligence is the monitoring and evaluation page for the fraud detection system. It is mainly useful for risk managers, model owners, and technical fraud specialists.

It shows:

- How accurately the system identifies fraud.
- How many legitimate transactions are incorrectly flagged.
- How much fraud is missed.
- How well the system separates legitimate and fraudulent activity.
- Monthly fraud volume and estimated exposure.
- The balance between behavioral fraud and traditional transaction fraud.
- The most important warning indicators used in decisions.
- How velocity, structuring, and recipient risk are distributed.
- A confusion matrix showing correct and incorrect classifications.

This page helps the organization understand whether the fraud detection service is reliable and which behaviors are driving alerts.

3.5 Inference Lab

The Inference Lab is a testing and simulation workspace. It allows an analyst to enter or modify transaction details and immediately see how the system would assess the scenario.

An analyst can change:

- Sender and recipient accounts.
- Transaction amount.
- Transaction channel.
- Sender and recipient balances.
- Recent transaction counts.
- Recent transaction amounts.
- Other behavioral details related to the transfer.

The page includes example scenarios such as:

- A large account drain.
- A sudden burst of transactions.
- Structuring or smurfing activity.
- Transfers to many recipients.
- A high-risk mule recipient.
- A normal retail transfer.

The result includes a risk score, a risk category, the main reasons for the result, and an indication of whether the recipient should be treated with caution.

The Inference Lab is useful for training analysts, testing unusual situations, checking proposed transactions, and demonstrating how different transaction details affect risk.

3.6 Risk Policies

Risk Policies is the configuration page for fraud review rules. It allows authorized users to adjust how risk levels are assigned and when additional action is required.

Users can configure:

- The critical-risk threshold for automatic escalation.
- The high-risk threshold for analyst review.
- The medium-risk threshold for additional verification.
- The rule that flags an account emptied to zero.
- The rule that escalates high-value wire transfers.
- The rule that applies a short response time to rapid beneficiary transfers.
- The API connection used by the application.

The page also provides a diagnostic check to confirm that the application services are available. Saving policy settings allows the organization to align the application with its current fraud response procedures.

4. How the Application Helps an Analyst

The application combines several kinds of evidence when reviewing a transaction:

- The transaction's predicted likelihood of fraud.
- The size and possible financial impact of the transaction.
- Whether the sender's account was drained.
- Whether the transaction channel is commonly associated with fraud.
- How quickly transactions are being made.
- Whether many recipients are being used in a short time.
- Whether the recipient has a suspicious or high-risk history.
- Whether the transaction resembles known fraud patterns.

The result is presented as a risk score and risk level. The system also provides reasons so that an analyst can understand the warning and make an accountable decision.

5. Typical User Workflow

1. The analyst opens the Executive Overview to check current alerts, exposure, and queue size.
2. The analyst opens the Fraud Triage Queue and filters or sorts cases by urgency.
3. The analyst selects a suspicious case to open the Case Investigator page.
4. The analyst reviews the transaction amount, account balances, recipient information, behavior, and risk reasons.
5. The analyst chooses an action: freeze the account, resolve the case, mark it as a false positive, or escalate it.
6. The analyst records notes or assigns the case when more investigation is needed.
7. The team monitors decisions and fraud trends through the Model Intelligence page.
8. Analysts can use the Inference Lab to test similar transactions or train new team members.
9. Risk managers can update thresholds and operating rules in Risk Policies.
10. Transaction and case information can be exported for reporting, audit, and compliance activities.

6. Business Value

Aegis helps financial organizations respond to suspicious activity faster and more consistently. It reduces the time needed to find important cases, gives analysts evidence for their decisions, creates a record of case actions, and helps protect accounts and customers from financial loss.

The application is not only a fraud detector. It is a complete fraud operations workspace for monitoring, investigation, decision-making, simulation, policy control, and reporting.
